# The Warm Handoff, and Why It Beats Today

A one-page guide for frontline responders, plus how this improves on what gets captured now.

## What's captured today vs. on the card

| Today (status quo) | Warm Handoff card |
|---|---|
| | **At the moment of contact** |
| Nothing standardized. A verbal "try that shelter" or a scribbled number. | **A structured next step** anyone can produce in two minutes, no HMIS login. |
| Availability assumed. People sent to full or closed services. | **Confirmation + freshness.** "Confirm before travel; capacity not live." |
| No one owns what happens next. Handoff drops when the responder leaves. | **Named owner + follow-up window.** Accountability is a person. |
| Handoff is anonymous and untracked. No way to know they arrived. | **First name + handoff code + ahead-call** link sender and receiver. |
| The ride is captured nowhere. The last mile fails silently. | **Transportation is a required field.** A bed with no ride isn't a plan. |
| Person re-tells their whole story at every door. | **Portable summary** travels with the person. No repeated intake. |
| Assessment tools may score or label the person; records persist. | **Minimum identity, no scoring, no biometrics,** not retained. |

## How to run a handoff (responder steps)

**Safety and medical first.** Immediate danger, medical emergency, or dangerous weather with no shelter? Call 911 or the crisis line and, if you can, transport now. Don't issue a card for an active emergency.

1. **Ask what they need, in their words.** Get their OK to help find a safe next step.
   - *"What would help most right now? Can I help you find somewhere to go?"*

2. **Capture the minimum.** First name + last initial, the immediate need, the eligibility lane (family, DV, youth, veteran, medical), and any accommodation (service animal, wheelchair, children). Nothing about their history.

3. **Pick the safe next step and confirm by phone.** Is there room, are they eligible, is a form required. The tool never claims a bed is open.

4. **Arrange the ride.** Van, bus pass, voucher, or accompany them. Don't send someone off with no way to get there.

5. **Call ahead.** Give the receiving agency the first name + handoff code so they expect the person.

6. **Hand off what the person keeps.** Their copy, or (no printer) write or say the field script: *where to go, the code, who's expecting them.* Explain what will happen.

7. **Owner follows up** within the window to confirm the person was received.

## How much to capture: the dignity line

### ✓ Capture (need & routing)
- Immediate need
- Eligibility lane (family, DV, youth, veteran, medical)
- Accommodations that change the door (service animal, mobility, children)
- First name + last initial
- Transportation + consent

### ✕ Don't capture (the person's story)
- Why they became homeless
- Medical or mental-health history
- Criminal history or "risk"
- Trauma narrative
- Full legal name, SSN, ID, photo, fingerprint

**The rule:** capture the need, not the person's story. Add detail only when it changes where they go, never to build a file. More questions is not more help — it's more burden. Ask, don't interrogate.
